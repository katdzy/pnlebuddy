## Normal values to know cold

| | Normal | Low (hypo-) | High (hyper-) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sodium (Na⁺) | 135–145 mEq/L | Confusion, headache, seizures, muscle cramps | Thirst, dry sticky mucosa, restlessness, agitation |
| Potassium (K⁺) | 3.5–5.0 mEq/L | Muscle weakness, leg cramps, flat T wave, **U wave** | Muscle weakness, **peaked T**, wide QRS, dysrhythmias |
| Calcium (Ca²⁺) | 8.5–10.5 mg/dL | Tetany, **Chvostek**, **Trousseau**, numbness/tingling | Muscle weakness, constipation, kidney stones, confusion |
| Magnesium (Mg²⁺) | 1.5–2.5 mg/dL | Tremors, hyperreflexia, seizures (like low Ca) | Hyporeflexia, flushing, bradycardia, respiratory depression |
| Phosphorus | 2.5–4.5 mg/dL | Weakness | Rises with low Ca (they move opposite) |

## Fluid volume

- **Deficit (FVD):** weight loss, tachycardia, weak pulse, low BP, dry mucosa, concentrated urine, ↑ hematocrit and BUN.
- **Excess (FVE):** weight gain, bounding pulse, high BP, crackles, distended neck veins, edema, ↓ hematocrit.
- **Daily weight** is the most reliable measure of fluid change: 1 kg ≈ 1 L of fluid.

## IV fluids

- **Isotonic** (stays in the vessels): 0.9% NaCl (NSS), Lactated Ringer's. D5W is isotonic in the bag but acts hypotonic once the dextrose is used.
- **Hypotonic** (moves into cells): 0.45% NaCl. Avoid in increased intracranial pressure.
- **Hypertonic** (pulls water out of cells): 3% NaCl, D5NSS, D10W. Watch for fluid overload.

## Potassium safety

- **Never give KCl IV push.** Always diluted, by infusion pump.
- Check urine output before giving potassium: kidneys must be working.
- Hyperkalemia emergency: calcium gluconate (protects the heart), insulin with dextrose and sodium bicarbonate (shift K into cells), kayexalate or dialysis (remove it).

## Acid-base in one table

| | pH | PaCO₂ (35–45) | HCO₃⁻ (22–26) | Common causes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Respiratory acidosis | ↓ < 7.35 | ↑ | normal / ↑ if compensating | COPD, hypoventilation, sedation |
| Respiratory alkalosis | ↑ > 7.45 | ↓ | normal / ↓ | Hyperventilation, anxiety, high altitude |
| Metabolic acidosis | ↓ | normal / ↓ | ↓ | DKA, renal failure, diarrhea |
| Metabolic alkalosis | ↑ | normal / ↑ | ↑ | Vomiting, NG suction, antacids |

**ROME:** Respiratory Opposite (pH and PaCO₂ move in opposite directions), Metabolic Equal (pH and HCO₃ move the
same way).

## Board-style traps

- "Which is the **priority**?" with hyperkalemia: the heart (cardiac monitoring) comes before everything else.
- Chvostek = tap the facial nerve; Trousseau = BP cuff on the arm. Both mean **low calcium** (or low magnesium).
- A client on furosemide (Lasix) loses potassium; a client on spironolactone keeps it.
